Ask us which aircraft suits a regional brief and, more often than not, the answer is the King Air. It is not the fastest thing we can put in the air, and it is not the flashiest. It is the one that lands where you actually need to go.

## Strips that jets cannot use

Most of Australia's regional and remote airstrips are short, and many are unsealed. A light jet needs sealed bitumen and a good deal of it. The King Air does not. It will operate from gravel and dirt strips a few hundred metres shorter than a jet requires, which means it reaches the stations, mine sites, communities and lodges that other aircraft simply cannot.

## The range to cross the country in a hop

With a stage length of around 1,800 kilometres, the King Air covers most regional legs without stopping to refuel. Brisbane to Longreach, Perth to Broome, Adelaide to the Flinders — these are single hops, flown at a height where the country is still worth looking at.

## Room for a small group, in comfort

The cabin seats up to eight guests and is pressurised and air-conditioned, so you arrive rested rather than rattled. For a family, a board, or a touring group, it is the sweet spot — private, direct, and sized for the job.

The King Air is not always the answer. For long interstate legs with larger numbers, a jet earns its keep. For a short run between capitals, scheduled services are hard to beat on cost. But for regional and remote Australia, in small groups, it is the aircraft we reach for first.
